Intro to ITWS

Course Description:
This course introduces students to the field of information technology and Web science, the types of problems encountered in the field, and the solution approaches used to solve them. Through a series of activities and projects, students are introduced to topics such as Web systems design, emerging Web standards, database systems, security, and computer networking. Guest speakers highlight information technology practices in industry. Students work in groups on a team project and presentation at the end of the course.

The Sociology of Zombies

Course Description:
This course introduces students to the sociological study of extreme events through the lens of zombie narratives, using films, literature, and media to examine how society responds when institutions collapse. Students explore themes such as social order, inequality, group behavior, deviance, and the ethics of survival, connecting fictional scenarios to real-world research on disasters and pandemics. Class activities include case studies, simulations, and media analysis, supported by guest speakers from emergency management and behavioral sciences. Working in teams, students conclude the course by developing a comprehensive societal response plan and presenting their assessment of how communities might function during a hypothetical zombie outbreak.

Clown Theory

Course Description:
This course offers an academic introduction to clowning as a global performance tradition, tracing the evolution of clown figures from ancient comedic rituals and court jesters to circus performers, theatrical clowns, and modern cultural interpretations. Students study how clowns use humor, satire, physical comedy, and exaggerated identity to challenge social norms and evoke both joy and discomfort. Through readings, media analysis, and hands-on performance activities, the course examines the psychology of humor, the cultural meaning of “the fool,” and the rise of subversive clown imagery in contemporary film and digital culture. Guest performers provide insight into professional clown practice, and students complete a collaborative performance or research presentation demonstrating their understanding of clown theory in action.
